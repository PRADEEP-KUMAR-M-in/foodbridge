# FOODBRIDGE simulation report

> **SIMULATION, NOT A MEASUREMENT.** Every probability and delay below is an assumption chosen by the developers. The results show what the design could do if the assumptions held. Only a real pilot can show what it does. Generated by `python run_simulation.py 200` (seed 1); re-running gives identical numbers.

## What is compared
- **Today:** a donor broadcasts to every organisation; the first "yes" gets it; a confirmation call follows; a no-show is noticed only after a manual wait; misfits (wrong storage or capacity) are often found late, so trips are wasted.
- **FOODBRIDGE:** hard constraint filters (storage, capacity, safe window including travel), ranked offers using the app's real scoring code (`app/matching.py`), a short first-dibs window per organisation, in-app instant claim, and automatic claim expiry with the next organisation offered.
- Every simulated week uses the same organisations and listings for every process, so rows are fairly compared.

## What the numbers say
- Wasted trips per 100 listings: 15.9 with today's process versus 0.0 with FOODBRIDGE.
- Share of listed food delivered (median of 200 simulated weeks): 87% today versus 87% with FOODBRIDGE. Week by week FOODBRIDGE was higher in 83, equal in 29 and lower in 88 of 200 weeks, so this model does NOT show that FOODBRIDGE rescues more food.
- Median minutes to a confirmed claim: 18 today versus 18 with FOODBRIDGE using a 2-minute first-dibs window; broadcasting to eligible organisations only took 13.
- Window sweep: the fastest setting tested was 'broadcast to eligible' (13 min to a claim); the longest window tested, 'first dibs 15 min', took 45 min and delivered 76%.
- Largest organisation's share of delivered food: 31% today versus 29% with FOODBRIDGE.
- In a world friendlier to today's process (fewer misfit replies, fast confirmation, few no-shows) the delivered share was 88% today versus 88% with FOODBRIDGE, and minutes to a claim were 13 versus 17.

## What this model does not show
- Real response times, real no-show rates and real donor behaviour (all assumed). The pilot must measure them; the first-dibs window should then be set from measured response times.
- Declared storage and capacity are assumed accurate.
- Donor effort, volunteers and vehicles, app adoption, network problems and the faster listing from Speak-to-List are not modelled.
- Double-dispatch in today's process (two organisations sent for the same food) is not modelled; leaving it out favours today's process.
- Food safety is not modelled; FOODBRIDGE never certifies food.
- Fairness is measured only as the largest organisation's share of delivered food.
